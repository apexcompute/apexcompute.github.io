Finite Elements from Scratch · Episode 2

The weak form: why we rewrite the heat equation

An aluminum bar, 4 cm long, makes 80 W of heat along its length; both ends are held at 20 °C. A broken line of two straight pieces can't go into its equation. Rewrite the equation, and it can. Here you find the middle temperature yourself, by balancing one hat.

1. Guess first

The exact temperature is a parabola, 40 °C in the middle. We look for the temperature only among broken lines: 20 °C at both ends, an unknown T2 at the middle node, straight in between. The test function is the middle hat N2: 1 at node 2, 0 at both ends. Work out T2 from the weak form with N2. Compared with the exact 40 °C, which do you trust most?

2. Balance the middle hat

With the middle hat as the test function, the weak form ∫ kA T′ N2′ dx = ∫ q N2 dx becomes one line. The left side is the heat flowing away from node 2 by the slopes of the two pieces: kA/h = 1 W/K per piece, times the temperature difference across it. The right side is the heat the hat collects: each element makes 40 W, and the hat takes half of it, so 20 + 20 = 40 W. Move T2 until the two sides agree.

The broken line matches the exact curve at the node, but not between the nodes: at x = 1 cm the broken line gives 30 °C and the exact solution 35 °C. The next episode cuts the bar finer and looks at that error.

What this episode shows

  • The original equation −kA T″ = q needs a second derivative; a broken line has none at its kink, so it can never satisfy the equation point by point.
  • Multiply by a test function, integrate, move one derivative with integration by parts: the weak form needs only first derivatives, and a broken line fits. Nothing is approximated yet.
  • The approximation is choosing broken lines for both the temperature and the test functions. Each element's 2×2 small table is kA/h × [1, −1; −1, 1], exactly like a spring's.

Run it yourself

One NumPy script checks every number of the pen-and-paper work; each integral is done by a hand formula and numerically, and prints PASS when they agree.

python3 weak_e02.py
python3 weak_e02.py --t-right 40     # the exercise below

Code and README on GitHub

Exercise: hold the right end at 40 °C instead. Work out T2 by hand first: 1 × (T2 − 20) + 1 × (T2 − 40) = 40. (You can also switch the right end in the figure above.)

T2 = 50 °C, and the exact solution at x = 2 cm is also 50 °C, so the node matches again. The heat leaving the ends becomes 50 W at the left and 30 W at the right, still 80 W in total.
